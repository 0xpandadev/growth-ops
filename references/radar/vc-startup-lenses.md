## Core Questions

- What changed?
- Why now?
- Who has urgent pain?
- What is the narrow first customer?
- What is the manual-first version?
- What is the wedge?
- What expands after the wedge?
- What makes distribution possible?
- What would make this a tarpit?
- What would prove PMF is emerging?

## YC Essential Advice Lens

Default to concrete behavior:

- launch something small
- talk to actual users
- do manual work before automation
- narrow the first customer segment
- avoid fake progress such as decks, branding, and vague research when no customer has reacted

Output implication:

- define a 7-day user conversation or manual delivery test
- define the exact user to contact
- define what reaction would count as real pull

## YC RFS Lens

Use RFS as a fertile problem-space pattern:

- What structural change makes this problem newly solvable?
- Who feels the problem before the market has a name for it?
- What ugly, specific first version could be built manually?
- What would make the opportunity a tarpit despite being fashionable?

Do not copy a hot RFS topic. Convert it into a concrete wedge and buyer.

## PMF Archetypes

Use Sequoia's public PMF framing as a diagnostic lens:

- `Hair on Fire`: customers know the pain is urgent and compare alternatives. Win through differentiated experience, speed, and GTM execution.
- `Hard Fact`: customers accept the status quo as unavoidable. Win by reframing the pain and educating the market.
- `Future Vision`: customers do not yet believe the new world is possible. Win by finding commercial pit stops and building belief over time.

Do not force every idea into "urgent pain". Some opportunities are behavior-change or belief-change problems.

## PMF Signals

Look for:

- repeated pull from a narrow segment
- willingness to pay or switch
- high retention or repeated use
- users hacking together alternatives
- strong word-of-mouth within a niche
- urgent workflow pain

## Tarpit Warnings

- everyone says it is useful, nobody pays
- buyer/user mismatch
- impossible data access
- regulated workflow without trust path
- too broad initial product
- no distribution channel

## Growth And Defensibility

Use Reforge and NFX-style public patterns when relevant:

- Prefer growth loops over one-way funnels: output from one cycle should feed the next cycle.
- Identify the loop input, action, output, reinvestment mechanism, and metric.
- Network effects matter only when product value increases as more users, suppliers, data, or developers join.
- Do not claim a network effect unless the mechanism is explicit.

## Network Effect Types To Consider

- direct network effect
- two-sided marketplace
- data network effect
- social graph
- protocol / standard
- developer ecosystem
- supply aggregation

For each, state the cold-start problem and why the loop would compound.
