# Market Intelligence

Use this for market maps, category analysis, technology radar, competitor landscapes, and private-company style research.

## Public Method Sources

Use public patterns from:

- CB Insights: market maps, private-company health, Mosaic-style signals
  - https://www.cbinsights.com/mosaic-score/
  - https://www.cbinsights.com/private-company-health
- SignalFire / Harmonic: startup and private-market signal models
- PitchBook / Dealroom / Crunchbase: funding, M&A, private-company data.
- Gartner Hype Cycle: maturity and expectation curve
  - https://www.gartner.com/en/research/methodologies/gartner-hype-cycle
- Porter Five Forces:
  - https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx
- McKinsey Three Horizons:
  - use as a growth-portfolio lens, not as a claim of proprietary access

## Market Map Axes

Choose axes that reveal buyer behavior:

- buyer type
- workflow
- budget owner
- business model
- deployment model
- maturity stage
- geography
- regulated vs unregulated
- SMB vs enterprise
- horizontal vs vertical

## CB Insights-Style Private Company Health

When judging private companies or emerging categories, use a multi-signal table rather than one story.

| Lens | What to look for | Example signals |
|---|---|---|
| Money | financial strength and viability | funding quality, runway proxy, revenue proxy, burn risk, investor quality |
| Market | category and industry health | market expansion, regulation, budget formation, buyer urgency, category tailwinds |
| Momentum | company trajectory | web traffic, product launches, news sentiment, integrations, customer wins, hiring velocity |
| Management | team execution potential | founder background, operator hires, prior exits, domain expertise, leadership churn |
| HR / hiring | internal growth signal | role mix, seniority, geography, engineering/sales/ops hiring pattern |
| Product awareness | external recognition | search interest, reviews, community mentions, analyst/category inclusion |
| Competitive pressure | durability risk | direct competitors, substitutes, pricing pressure, platform dependency |

Do not call this "Mosaic" scoring unless using CB Insights data directly. Call it a Mosaic-inspired signal stack.

## SignalFire / Harmonic / PitchBook / Dealroom Discipline

Use private-market databases as a reference model for what to look for, not as something this skill can fully reproduce.

Signal stack:

- founding team and operator quality
- headcount and function-level hiring changes
- funding round, investor quality, and financing recency
- web traffic / product usage proxies
- customer type and market segment
- category tags and competitive cluster
- partnerships, integrations, and platform dependency
- M&A, shutdown, down-round, or distress signals

For every signal, label it:

- `leading`: may predict change
- `lagging`: confirms what already happened
- `ambiguous`: needs corroboration
- `missing`: cannot be verified with available tools

## Gartner Hype Cycle Discipline

Use Hype Cycle logic to avoid chasing fashionable themes.

Classify the theme:

- innovation trigger
- peak of inflated expectations
- trough of disillusionment
- slope of enlightenment
- plateau of productivity

Then translate to action:

- build now
- monitor
- sell implementation / cleanup
- wait for buyer education
- avoid unless there is a niche urgent use case

## Porter Five Forces Discipline

For markets, do not stop at TAM. Check profit structure:

- buyer power
- supplier power
- threat of substitutes
- threat of new entrants
- rivalry among existing players

Output should include where margins get compressed and where a small operator can still win.

## McKinsey Three Horizons Discipline

Use Three Horizons for portfolio balance:

- H1: improve and defend the current core
- H2: scale adjacent opportunities
- H3: explore future options

For small companies, translate this into resource reality:

- H1 pays the bills
- H2 gets a weekly owner and measurable test
- H3 stays small until evidence improves

## Output Pattern

1. Category definition
2. Market map axes
3. Player clusters
4. Buyer criteria
5. Adoption triggers
6. Risks and contradictions
7. Watchlist
8. Next validation

## Avoid

- market maps that are just logo collections
- funding as proof of customer demand
- analyst vocabulary without buyer pain
- single-source category conclusions
