# Classic Strategy And Management Methods

Use this file when the user needs industry structure, strategy, portfolio allocation, organizational alignment, execution KPIs, or customer/operating improvement.

The goal is not to name frameworks. The goal is to turn classic MBA and modern management methods into decision fields, tests, and actions.

## Selection Rule

Pick the smallest set that changes the decision.

```text
External structure -> PESTEL, Five Forces, strategic groups, profit pools
Internal advantage -> value chain, VRIO/RBV, generic strategy, capability gaps
Portfolio allocation -> BCG growth-share, GE/McKinsey matrix, Three Horizons
Execution alignment -> 7S, Balanced Scorecard, change management
Customer/operating improvement -> segmentation, journey analysis, benchmarking
Uncertainty -> scenario analysis, signposts, no-regret moves, options
```

If a framework does not produce an output field, metric, falsifier, or next action, do not use it.

## Method Cards

### PESTEL

Use for macro and external-context pressure.

Ask:
- What political, economic, social, technological, environmental, or legal change matters?
- Which factor changes customer budget, operating cost, compliance burden, supply, or timing?
- Is this a tailwind, headwind, constraint, or trigger?

Output fields:
- external factor
- business impact
- affected stakeholder
- time horizon
- watch signal
- action or hedge

Trap:
- Listing macro trends without linking them to demand, cost, risk, or timing.

### Porter Five Forces

Use for industry attractiveness and profit-pool pressure.

Ask:
- Which force captures value away from the company?
- Are buyers powerful because they are concentrated, price-sensitive, undifferentiated, or easy to switch?
- Are suppliers powerful because they control scarce inputs, talent, distribution, data, or platforms?
- Are substitutes solving the same job in a different way?
- Are entry barriers real or imagined?
- Does rivalry destroy margin through price, fixed-cost pressure, or slow growth?

Output fields:
- force
- pressure level
- evidence
- profit impact
- strategic response
- watchpoint

Trap:
- Saying a market is attractive because it is large while ignoring who keeps the margin.

### Value Chain

Use for cost, differentiation, and operating-model diagnosis.

Ask:
- Where is value created, lost, delayed, or duplicated?
- Which activity drives customer value but is underinvested?
- Which activity consumes cost without increasing willingness to pay?
- Which activity could be standardized, automated, outsourced, or made proprietary?

Output fields:
- activity
- current cost / pain
- customer value
- bottleneck
- improvement lever
- owner
- KPI

Trap:
- Treating the value chain as a diagram instead of a cost/value map.

### VRIO / Resource-Based View

Use for durable advantage and capability assessment.

Ask:
- Is the resource valuable, rare, hard to imitate, and organized to capture value?
- Is the advantage a real capability or just an asset?
- What would make competitors catch up?

Output fields:
- resource / capability
- V/R/I/O score
- proof
- weakness
- investment needed
- moat risk

Trap:
- Calling brand, data, community, or know-how a moat without evidence of rarity or capture.

### Generic Strategy And Strategic Groups

Use when strategy is confused or competition is poorly framed.

Ask:
- Is the company competing on cost, differentiation, focus, or an unstable mixture?
- Which competitors share the same buyer, price band, channel, scope, and capability base?
- Where is the white space or over-served segment?

Output fields:
- chosen basis of competition
- tradeoff
- direct competitors
- adjacent competitors
- white-space segment
- strategic inconsistency

Trap:
- Recommending both low cost and high customization without naming the tradeoff.

### BCG Growth-Share / GE-McKinsey Portfolio Logic

Use for products, business units, initiatives, or investment allocation.

Ask:
- Which units deserve investment, harvesting, fixing, or exit?
- Is market growth attractive enough to justify resources?
- Is relative competitive strength high enough to win?
- Is cash generation funding future options or hiding decline?

Output fields:
- unit / initiative
- market attractiveness
- competitive position
- cash role
- investment action
- exit / hold / scale decision

Trap:
- Using the matrix as a label instead of resource allocation.

### McKinsey Three Horizons

Use for growth portfolio timing.

Ask:
- Is this core improvement, adjacent expansion, or future option?
- What governance and metrics fit the horizon?
- What should be protected from core-business operating cadence?

Output fields:
- H1 / H2 / H3 classification
- resource level
- owner
- metric
- review cadence
- kill / scale trigger

Trap:
- Running uncertain H3 bets with H1 ROI expectations.

### McKinsey 7S

Use for transformation, reorganization, modernization, and change failure diagnosis.

Ask:
- Are strategy, structure, systems, skills, staff, style, and shared values aligned?
- Which S is blocking execution?
- Is the failure caused by org design, incentives, talent, routines, or culture?

Output fields:
- 7S element
- current state
- required state
- gap
- action
- owner
- adoption risk

Trap:
- Treating transformation as a strategy deck problem when systems and incentives remain unchanged.

### Balanced Scorecard

Use for execution management and KPI design.

Ask:
- What financial, customer, internal process, innovation/learning, employee, or ESG measure proves progress?
- Which metric is leading vs lagging?
- What target, owner, and review rhythm are needed?

Output fields:
- strategic objective
- perspective
- KPI
- baseline
- target
- owner
- cadence
- red/yellow/green threshold

Trap:
- Creating too many KPIs with no decision rights or operating cadence.

### Customer Segmentation And Journey Analysis

Use for go-to-market, CX, sales, product, churn, and service redesign.

Ask:
- Which customer segment has the strongest need, willingness to pay, reachability, and repeatability?
- What episode does the customer experience from trigger to outcome?
- Where are the moments of friction, waste, delay, confusion, or trust loss?
- Which touchpoint changes conversion, retention, cost-to-serve, or NPS-like advocacy?

Output fields:
- segment
- job / episode
- touchpoint
- pain / waste
- business metric affected
- fix
- expected impact

Trap:
- Building around internal silos rather than customer episodes.

### Scenario Analysis And Contingency Planning

Use for uncertainty, investment timing, turnaround, pricing, AI adoption, cash, regulation, or market-entry decisions.

Ask:
- What are the 2-3 critical uncertainties?
- What scenarios are extreme but plausible?
- Which moves are no-regret?
- Which moves are big bets, hedges, or options?
- Which signposts should trigger a decision change?

Output fields:
- scenario
- assumptions
- operating impact
- financial impact
- no-regret move
- hedge / option
- trigger point

Trap:
- Producing three generic cases without signposts or actions.

### Change Management

Use when execution requires behavior change.

Ask:
- Who must change behavior?
- What resistance is rational from their perspective?
- What communication, training, incentive, process, or governance change is required?
- What early warning signal shows adoption is failing?

Output fields:
- stakeholder
- required behavior
- friction
- enablement
- risk mitigation
- adoption metric
- escalation path

Trap:
- Assuming agreement with a plan means the organization can execute it.

## Practical Output Pattern

When using classic strategy methods, produce this compact block:

```text
Decision:
Relevant method(s):
Why this method fits:
Key diagnostic finding:
Output fields changed:

Falsifier:
```
